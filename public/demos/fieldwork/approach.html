Field guide / 04 / Approach

First, a better set of questions.

A good process gives the garden a direction before it gives it a list of materials.

The process

From everyday life to a landscape plan.

The sequence below describes a possible working approach for this fictional studio. A real appointment and deliverables would be agreed separately.

01

Listen and observe

Describe the garden as it is now. Talk about the moments you want more of, the parts you avoid, and the practical constraints you know.

Tell us about your garden ↗
02

Shape the brief

Prioritise circulation, gathering, privacy, and planting. Establish what the design should solve and what is outside the agreed work.

Build your brief ↗
03

Explore a direction

Use a plan and simple visual references to test a coherent spatial idea. Discuss the choices while they are still easy to change.

See concept studies ↗
04

Resolve and hand over

Bring the agreed direction into the drawing detail needed for the next step. Confirm what belongs with the studio and what calls for a separate contractor or specialist.

Practical questions ↗

How we think

Design for the whole year.

A garden changes in small increments. We use that movement as part of the design.

Rhythm

Repeat a useful idea

A few recurring forms can bring coherence without making every part of the garden identical.

Season

Make room for change

Structure holds a garden together while flowers, foliage, and light take turns in the foreground.

Care

Be realistic early

A garden works best when its maintenance needs fit the attention someone can give it.
