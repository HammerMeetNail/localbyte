Fieldwork / Field notes

Preparing for a garden design conversation.

A small field guide for turning a collection of garden ideas into a useful brief.

Before a conversation

Bring observations, not a perfect plan.

You do not need design vocabulary. A few specific examples from daily life can be more useful than a folder full of images.

01 / Look

Notice light and routes

Take photographs from the house and from the garden. Observe where the sun reaches, where water gathers, and which routes you already take.

02 / Describe

Name the useful moments

Write down three things you would like to do in the garden: eat outside, find a quiet corner, grow herbs, or make a clear entrance.

03 / Prioritise

Separate needs and wishes

Decide which problems must be solved, which ideas are flexible, and which choices need advice before a direction can be agreed.

A planting brief

Four details that help.

A planting plan responds to a place. Start with these observations and leave unknowns marked as questions.

Exposure

Note whether the area feels sunny, shaded, sheltered, or windy at the times you use it. The pattern can differ across a small garden.

Existing planting

Identify what you want to keep and what feels unsuccessful. Photographs are helpful when plant names are unfamiliar.

Ground conditions

Record what you already know about the soil and drainage. A design conversation can identify which observations need further checking.

Care and character

Describe the amount of tending you want to do and the feeling you prefer: loose, structured, quiet, colourful, or a combination.
