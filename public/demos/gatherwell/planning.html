Gatherwell / Planning

A good gathering starts simply.

A practical outline for turning an initial idea into the questions worth asking.

The planning notes

Four steps to a useful brief.

You can begin with a rough idea. The first conversation helps identify what needs a decision and what needs more information.

01

Describe the occasion

Write a sentence about the purpose of the day and the feeling you want people to leave with. Add an approximate guest count and a preferred period, if you know them.

Find a starting point ↗
02

Explore the setting

Think about the spaces you would like to use, the flow between them, and any access needs. The venue would need to confirm what is suitable.

Look through the spaces ↗
03

List the practical pieces

Food, equipment, setup, travel, and suppliers each need a clear owner. Keep questions visible until the responsibilities are agreed.

Read practical answers ↗
04

Agree the next conversation

A real inquiry would lead to an agreed next step, perhaps a visit or planning call through the venue’s existing scheduling tool.

Try the inquiry journey ↗

Before you inquire

A short checklist.

Bring the details you have. Mark the others as open questions.

People and purpose

Who is the day for? Roughly how many people may attend? Are there access or comfort requirements to raise early?

A preferred period

A month, season, or proposed date is useful context. Entering one on this website does not check or reserve availability.

The shape of the day

Describe an arrival, a main activity, and an ending. A brief outline is enough for a first discussion.

Questions for the venue

Ask about suitability, inclusions, suppliers, arrangements, and any limits that affect your plans.
